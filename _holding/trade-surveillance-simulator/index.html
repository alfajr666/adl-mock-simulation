Play an analyst on an exchange’s market-surveillance desk. You hold full account-level data, a queue of alerts, and a set of thresholds. Some alerts point at planted episodes. Many do not. Decide which is which, then check.

Three things it is built to show

Alerts are leads

The same rules fire on a planted ring and on two sub-accounts of one market maker moving inventory. Same-owner trades look like wash trades in form; only context separates them, and context takes a person.

Linkage does the real work

Look at accounts one at a time and a hidden group looks like four ordinary traders. Change how strictly accounts are linked and an episode can vanish, or dozens of honest accounts can merge into one group.

Every threshold is a trade

Tighten a limit and unrelated alerts fall, until real episodes fall with them. Sweep a setting, then check it across thirty seeds before trusting one run.

What the numbers mean

A planted scenario is always easier than a real one, so these numbers show how rules react to designed situations, not how well they would detect abuse on a real market. The scorecard counts cases per synthetic run (one per planted episode, designed trap or cluster of unrelated alerts), and with two planted episodes a single run says little: read the distribution across seeds. Both episodes are found with accounts named in nearly every seed at default settings, by design, so the informative figures are how many other cases surround them (the share of cases matching a planted episode has a median of about 0.29), how that moves with linkage, baseline and thresholds, and the shortcuts, such as register and tape tells, that a real desk does not get. Scripted crowds and a clean baseline period stay contrived.

How a session goes

  1. Scan the market tape and open an alert from the queue.
  2. Read its case file: price and volume, account register, link graph, supporting trades, group position and profit and loss.
  3. Record a decision: close, keep monitoring, or escalate.
  4. Reveal what was planted for that alert, or show the whole answer key for the case-level scorecard and the trade-off explorer.
